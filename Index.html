The Hot, Swollen Joint

A Practical Guide for UK Emergency Medicine

Assume Septic Arthritis

Until Proven Otherwise

Mortality is ~11%. Delayed treatment leads to rapid, irreversible joint destruction.

1. Clinical Suspicion

Red Flags

  • Acute onset single hot, swollen, painful joint
  • Markedly reduced active & passive movement
  • Systemic signs (fever, tachycardia, confusion)

High-Risk Factors

Elderly (>80) Diabetes RA / Immuno-suppressed Prosthetic Joint IVDU

2. The Critical Step: ASPIRATE

⚠️ MUST be performed BEFORE starting antibiotics

Unless the patient is in frank sepsis and aspiration will be significantly delayed.

Procedure Pearls

  • Strict aseptic technique (sterile gloves/field).
  • Aspirate to dryness (this provides pain relief).
  • Use Ultrasound guidance if available.

Contraindications & Safety

  • DO NOT aspirate a prosthetic joint (Urgent Ortho referral).
  • Anticoagulation is NOT a contraindication.
  • Do not aspirate through overlying cellulitis.

3. Investigations

Synovial Fluid (Priority)

  1. Gram Stain & MC&S
  2. WCC (>50,000/μL is highly suggestive)
  3. Polarised Microscopy (Crystals)

Bloods

  • Blood Cultures (x2 sets)
  • FBC, U&E, LFT
  • CRP / ESR (Baseline)
  • Urate (Optional - poor acute utility)

Imaging

  • X-Ray: Baseline, exclude fracture/chondrocalcinosis.
  • Ultrasound: Confirm effusion & guide needle.
  • MRI: Gold standard for Osteomyelitis.

4. Management

Empirical Antibiotics

Start immediately after cultures. Verify with local guidelines (Microguide/RxGuidelines).

Standard Cover
Flucloxacillin 2g IV QDS
Penicillin Allergy
Clindamycin 600mg IV QDS
High Risk (IVDU / Gram -ve)
Add Ceftriaxone 2g IV OD
MRSA Risk
Consider Vancomycin

Disposition

  • Admission: All suspected septic arthritis patients require admission.
  • Orthopaedics: Urgent referral for ALL cases.
  • Drainage: Daily needle aspiration or surgical washout (especially hips).

5. Clinical Pearls

  • Absence of fever does NOT exclude septic arthritis.
  • Negative Gram stain does NOT exclude septic arthritis.
  • Presence of crystals does NOT exclude co-existent infection.
  • Serum urate is useless in the acute setting (levels often drop during stress).
  • Prosthetic joints are complex—never stick a needle in them in ED.

Aspiration Quick Guides

Knee (Superolateral)

  • Position: Supine, knee slightly flexed.
  • Entry: 1cm superior & 1cm lateral to patella.
  • Aim: Inferomedially, under the patella.

Ankle (Anteromedial)

  • Position: Plantarflexion.
  • Entry: Medial to tibialis anterior, lateral to malleolus.
  • Aim: Posterior and superior into joint space.

Wrist (Dorsal)

  • Position: Pronated, slightly flexed.
  • Entry: Just ulnar to Lister's tubercle.
  • Aim: Perpendicular to skin.

Shoulder (Anterior)

  • Position: Sitting, arm adducted/externally rotated.
  • Entry: 1-2cm lateral to coracoid process.
  • Aim: Posteriorly towards glenoid.
